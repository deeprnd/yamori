

# V3.8: Lease Capitalization Adjustment

**Labels:** `type/epic`, `type/enhancement`, `area/finance`
**GitHub Issue:** TBD

See lease-adjusted debt and EBIT for a retail or airline company, revealing the true leverage and operating profitability behind the operating-lease-heavy business model. Compute the present value of operating lease obligations, adjust debt and EBIT consistently, and ensure the adjustment feeds every downstream metric that depends on debt or EBIT. Companies with significant operating leases appear to have lower leverage and higher EBIT than comparable companies that finance their facilities through debt. This adjustment corrects that by capitalizing operating leases, making cross-sector comparisons of leverage and ROIC fair.

## Product Intent

Let users see lease-adjusted debt and EBIT for a retail or airline company, revealing the true leverage and operating profitability behind the operating-lease-heavy business model. Compute the present value of operating lease obligations using the methodology's supplied discount rate, adjust debt and EBIT consistently, and ensure the adjustment feeds every downstream metric. The outcome is that companies with significant operating leases are valued on the same capital basis as companies with equivalent debt-financed assets — no structural bias toward asset-light models.

## Success Metrics

- Capitalized lease obligation: present value of operating lease payments using methodology's discount rate.
- Lease-adjusted debt: reported debt + capitalized lease obligation.
- Lease-adjusted EBIT: reported EBIT + lease expense − depreciation on capitalized lease.
- Lease-adjusted invested capital: includes capitalized lease obligation.
- Consistent treatment: lease adjustment applied to EBIT, debt, invested capital, and ROIC together.
- Every adjusted metric carries the adjustment provenance (capitalized amount, discount rate used).

## Demo Moment

- Command or flow: `tickoni valuation run <retail_company> --adjustment lease`
- Expected result: Lease-adjusted debt, EBIT, and invested capital shown alongside the unadjusted values, revealing the true leverage position.

### How Many Stories?

Four stories: capitalized obligation (S1), debt adjustment (S2), EBIT adjustment (S3), and consistency + provenance (S4). The scope is narrow (one adjustment type), so 4 stories is sufficient.

### Decomposition Rules

1. **Independent delivery**: Each story produces verifiable adjusted financial data.
2. **One capability per story**: S1 = obligation computation; S2 = debt adjustment; S3 = EBIT adjustment; S4 = consistency.
3. **Split by capability lifecycle**: S1 computes the adjustment amount; S2–S3 apply it; S4 verifies consistency.
4. **Closure story**: S4 ensures the adjustment is applied consistently across all dependent metrics.

### Bad Splits (Avoid)

- "Lease-adjusted debt" + "Lease-adjusted EBIT" as separate stories without consistency verification. They must be applied together.

### Story Line Format

Story IDs follow `VX.Y.SN`. Each story is independently compilable and testable.
