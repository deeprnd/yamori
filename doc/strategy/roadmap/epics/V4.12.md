# V4.12: Reverse DCF — Implied Growth from Market Price

**Labels:** `type/epic`, `type/feature`, `area/investing`
**GitHub Issue:** TBD

A DCF produces one number: the intrinsic value given your assumptions. But the market is also producing a number — the current stock price — and that price embeds the market's implied assumptions about future growth, margins, and risk. V4.12 solves for the Stage 1 growth rate that makes the DCF output equal the current market price using GSL's fixed-count bisection solver (100 iterations, not tolerance-based). It holds all other assumptions fixed. It returns the implied growth rate and corresponding intrinsic value. The outcome is that the analyst can answer: "What does the current market price imply about the company's growth assumptions, and is that implication supported by the company's fundamentals?" This transforms the DCF from a one-sided calculation into a two-way dialogue with the market.

## Product Intent

Deliver the reverse DCF engine: solve for the Stage 1 growth rate implied by the current market price using the exact fixed-iteration methodology in the prompt. The outcome is a single number — the implied growth rate — that answers whether the market is pricing in more or less growth than the fundamentals support. This is the most direct comparison between market price and intrinsic value: instead of comparing two absolute prices, compare two growth rates.

## Users And Jobs

- [Investment analyst]: See what growth rate the current market price implies and compare it to the fundamental growth rate derived from ROIC × reinvestment.
- [Compliance reviewer]: Verify that the bisection solver runs exactly 100 iterations (not tolerance-based) and that all other assumptions are held fixed.
- [Engineer]: Extend the reverse DCF by adding alternative solvers (Brent's method) as a policy switch, keeping the fixed-iteration guarantee.
- [Tickoni tile]: Consume `reverseDCF(market_price)` as a callable pivot that returns the implied growth rate.

## Success Metrics

- Reverse DCF solves for Stage 1 growth rate using GSL fixed-count bisection (100 iterations).
- All other assumptions held fixed during the solve.
- Implied growth rate returned with corresponding intrinsic value at that growth rate.
- Bit-identical output across all four target platforms (fixed-count, not tolerance-based).
- Solver converges for companies with positive fundamentals (positive ROIC, positive FCFF).
- Returns typed Error for companies where the solve is mathematically undefined (negative FCFF, negative ROIC with positive price).

## Demo Moment

- Command or flow: `tickoni valuation run <company> --reverse-dcf`
- Expected result: Implied Stage 1 growth rate, comparison with fundamental growth rate (ROIC × reinvestment), and interpretation of whether the market is pricing in more or less growth than fundamentals support.

## Scope

### In Scope

- GSL root solver: `gsl_root_fsolver_bisection` behind the reverse-DCF adapter.
- DCF objective callback: expose `DCF(g_high) − market_price` as the solver callback.
- Fixed 100 iterations: advance the GSL bisection solver exactly 100 times, no tolerance-based stopping.
- Implied growth result: return the solved Stage 1 growth rate and corresponding intrinsic value.
- Interpretation: compare implied growth to fundamental growth (ROIC × reinvestment).
- Error handling: undefined solve cases (negative FCFF, negative ROIC with positive price).

### How Many Stories?

Four stories: solver integration (S1), objective callback (S2), fixed iteration (S3), and result + interpretation (S4). The scope is narrow (one solver), so 4 stories is sufficient.

### Decomposition Rules

1. **Independent delivery**: Each story produces verifiable solver output.
2. **One capability per story**: S1 = solver; S2 = callback; S3 = iteration control; S4 = result.
3. **Split by capability lifecycle**: S1–S2 set up the solver; S3 controls execution; S4 produces output.
4. **Closure story**: S4 returns the solved growth rate with interpretation.

### Bad Splits (Avoid)

- "Solver integration" + "Objective callback" as two stories. The callback is part of the solver setup — they are one capability.

### Story Line Format

Story IDs follow `VX.Y.SN`. Each story is independently compilable and testable.
