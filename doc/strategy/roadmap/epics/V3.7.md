# V3.7: R&D Capitalization Adjustment

**Labels:** `type/epic`, `type/feature`, `area/investing`
**GitHub Issue:** TBD

Most large-cap companies — especially tech, pharma, and biotech — expense R&D rather than capitalizing it as an asset. Treating R&D as a pure expense understates both EBIT and invested capital, overstating the cost of equity and understating ROIC. V3.7 capitalizes R&D as an amortizing asset using sector-dependent amortization periods. It adjusts EBIT by adding back current-period R&D less amortization of the unamortized research asset. It adjusts invested capital by adding the unamortized research asset. This is Damodaran's signature adjustment and was entirely absent in M1's raw valuation. The outcome is that companies with material R&D spend receive the same capital treatment as companies with material physical capital — making ROIC and EBIT comparable across sectors.

## Product Intent

Deliver the R&D capitalization adjustment: compute the unamortized research asset from historical R&D spend using sector-dependent amortization periods, adjust EBIT and invested capital consistently, and ensure the adjustment feeds every downstream metric that depends on EBIT or invested capital. The outcome is that R&D-intensive companies are valued on the same capital basis as asset-intensive companies — no sector bias in ROIC or EBIT margins.

## Success Metrics

- Unamortized research asset computed from historical R&D series using sector-dependent amortization periods.
- R&D-adjusted EBIT: reported EBIT + current R&D − research-asset amortization.
- R&D-adjusted invested capital: reported invested capital + unamortized research asset.
- R&D adjustment applied consistently to EBIT, invested capital, and ROIC.
- Sector-dependent amortization periods: configurable per sector (e.g., software 3 years, pharma 7 years).
- Every adjusted metric carries the adjustment provenance (sector, amortization period, unamortized amount).
- No double-counting: R&D expense removed from income statement, asset added to balance sheet.

### How Many Stories?

Four stories: unamortized asset (S1), EBIT adjustment (S2), invested capital adjustment (S3), and consistency + provenance (S4). The scope is narrow (one adjustment type), so 4 stories is sufficient.

### Decomposition Rules

1. **Independent delivery**: Each story produces verifiable adjusted financial data.
2. **One capability per story**: S1 = asset computation; S2 = EBIT adjustment; S3 = capital adjustment; S4 = consistency.
3. **Split by capability lifecycle**: S1 computes the adjustment amount; S2–S3 apply it; S4 verifies consistency.
4. **Closure story**: S4 ensures the adjustment is applied consistently across all dependent metrics.

### Bad Splits (Avoid)

- "R&D-adjusted EBIT" + "R&D-adjusted invested capital" as separate stories without consistency verification. They must be applied together.

### Story Line Format

Story IDs follow `VX.Y.SN`. Each story is independently compilable and testable.
