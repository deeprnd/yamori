# M3: Financial Statement Ingestion — The Art of Accounting Interpretation

## Description

Financial statements are not clean numbers — they are narratives written in a standardized but deeply interpretable language. M3 extends M2's valuation engine with financial statement ingestion, XBRL mapping, accounting adjustments, base-year normalization, and source-priority resolution — the data integrity layer that ensures every input to the DCF is auditable and methodologically sound.

The logical approach to messy data is to clean it and move on. The psycho-logic insight is that the mess *is* the signal. R&D treatment, lease classification, SBC accounting — these aren't cosmetic differences. They are structural choices made by management that encode strategic reality. A company that capitalizes R&D isn't just using different accounting; it's making a different claim about the nature of its value creation. Yamori doesn't smooth over these choices — it interprets them deterministically, turning accounting subjectivity into policy-driven transparency.

```text
raw statements -> XBRL mapping -> adjustments -> normalized financials
```

M3 closes the gap between raw financial filings and the clean, adjusted inputs that M2's engine requires. It handles the most common source of valuation error: inconsistent accounting treatment across companies and periods. R&D capitalization, lease capitalization, stock-based compensation deduction, and base-year margin normalization are all implemented as deterministic policy-driven transformations on the dependency graph, not LLM judgments. Every adjusted metric carries provenance tracing to the source filing and specific adjustment applied.

## Library Dependencies

**Library: Apache Arrow C++ (CSV)** — XBRL filing ingestion and CSV-based statement normalization.

**Library: GNU GSL (Statistics)** — Historical medians for base-year normalization and distortion detection.

**Not used: Cuba.** M3 does not need Cuba because we are not numerically integrating ∫f(x)dx. The Monte Carlo sampling through DCF uses GSL RNG and distributions — no new dependency beyond M2.

**Not used: QuantLib, BLAS/LAPACK, TA-Lib, FFTW.** These are post-M4 additions, each introduced only when a domain requires capabilities the current backends do not provide.

## Included Epics

### Epic V3.6 — Financial statement ingestion and XBRL mapping

Normalizes financial statements from XBRL filings into Yamori's typed internal representation, mapping US-GAAP and IFRS elements to canonical names. Handles the filing structure (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, notes) and ensures every metric carries its source document, period, and currency. Each ingested metric becomes a named formula node on the dependency graph with explicit provenance.

### Epic V3.7 — R&D capitalization adjustment

Capitalizes R&D as an amortizing asset using sector-dependent amortization periods. Adjusts EBIT by adding back current-period R&D less amortization. Adjusts invested capital by adding the unamortized R&D asset. This is Damodaran's signature adjustment and was entirely absent in M2's raw valuation. Registered as a formula node that feeds NOPAT and invested capital.

### Epic V3.8 — Lease capitalization adjustment

Capitalizes operating lease obligations using the methodology's supplied discount rate. Adjusts debt by adding capitalized lease obligations. Adjusts EBIT by adding lease expense less depreciation on the capitalized lease. Ensures lease treatment is consistent across EBIT, invested capital, and ROIC.

### Epic V3.9 — Stock-based compensation and diluted shares

Deducts SBC as a real operating expense rather than adding it back. Values outstanding employee options using the Black-Scholes framework (or treasury-method fallback when option-level data is unavailable). Adjusts share count for option dilution.

### Epic V3.10 — Base-year normalization and distortion detection

Applies the five-year historical median margin to detect base-year distortion (current margin deviating >30% from median). Normalizes EBIT when distortion is detected. Validates that stage-1 inputs derive from history, not assertion. Reports the normalization decision in provenance.

### Epic V3.11 — TTM calculation and source-priority resolution

Handles trailing-twelve-month (TTM) calculations for metrics reported on a fiscal-year basis. Resolves source priority when multiple filings or data sources provide the same metric (10-K > 10-Q > earnings release > analyst consensus). Records which source and vintage was used.

## Why This Is A Milestone

M3 transforms M2 from a pure arithmetic engine into a production-ready valuation system that handles the messy reality of financial filings. Without M3's adjustments, every valuation would be methodologically incorrect for companies with material R&D, leases, or SBC — which includes the majority of large-cap firms. M4 and M5 depend on M3's adjusted inputs to produce valid cross-checks, peer comparisons, and Monte Carlo simulations.

Every adjustment is a deterministic policy-driven transformation on the dependency graph. The key insight: these adjustments don't just fix numbers — they reframe accounting choices into strategic signals. A company that capitalizes R&D isn't being manipulated; it's telling you it sees its R&D as an investment, not an expense. Yamori captures that intent deterministically.

A valuation is only as credible as its inputs — and credibility comes not from clean numbers, but from transparent interpretation. R&D becomes an asset, leases become debt, SBC becomes expense, and distorted base years get normalized. This is not cosmetic — these adjustments change every downstream metric and can swing intrinsic value by 30% or more. Yamori's commitment to grounded inputs means no adjustment is optional, and every adjustment is traceable.
